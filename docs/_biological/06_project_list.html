Testing fungal mycelium and fungal extracts against nematode eggs A260150-00 Jules Peter Ascaris suum (RG2), Toxocara canis (RG2), Pleurotus ostreatus, Ganoderma lucidum, Lentinula edodes, Pholiota nameko (all RG1) 2 CLA J26 02 Jun 2026 - submitted 26 May 2026, under consultation

Activity A260150-00: summary

Description. Development of a decontamination protocol for materials contaminated with parasitic nematode eggs. Isolated eggs of A. suum and T. canis are exposed in vitro to spent mushroom substrate extracts and slurries, fungal secondary metabolites, and acids at different concentrations, pH, temperatures (typically 25–37 °C), and incubation times. Egg viability is assessed by microscopy and vitality assays. Scale: very small (storage, 0–1 l) to small (1–10 l).

Activity steps. (1) reception and storage of eggs at 4 °C; (2) preparation of test materials; (3) egg exposure in tubes or multi-well plates inside the BSC; (4) monitoring and sampling; (5) decontamination and waste management by autoclaving; (6) documentation. The working procedure is described in the SOP.

Risk assessment (summary).

  • Hazard: both species are Risk Group 2 zoonotic parasites. Humans can be infected only by ingesting embryonated eggs; the eggs cannot multiply in the environment or in the human body.
  • Exposure route: accidental ingestion (hand-to-mouth). Inhalation risk is minimal because eggs are kept in liquid suspension; skin contact is negligible.
  • Embryonation: eggs are received non-embryonated, but untreated controls and insufficiently treated eggs incubated at 25–37 °C can embryonate within a few weeks and become infectious. Therefore, all eggs are handled as potentially infectious at all times.
  • Severity: infection is treatable (albendazole for T. canis, mebendazole for A. suum); toxocariasis can cause visceral or ocular larva migrans in severe cases.
  • Measures: all open handling in the BSC, gloves and lab coat, no hand-to-face contact, mechanical removal of spills, inactivation of all egg-containing material by autoclaving, annual instruction of staff.
  • Conclusion: moderate occupational risk; BSL2 containment is appropriate and sufficient. No environmental risk is expected.
